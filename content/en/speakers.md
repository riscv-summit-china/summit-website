  - id: david-patterson
    name: David A. Patterson
    position: Professor Emeritus, UC Berkeley
    summary: "Turing Award laureate and a co-creator of the RISC and RISC-V instruction set architectures, and an authoritative scholar in computer architecture."
    bio: "David A. Patterson received the 2017 ACM A.M. Turing Award with John Hennessy and is a co-creator of the RISC and RISC-V instruction set architectures, as well as an authoritative scholar in computer architecture. As Professor Emeritus at UC Berkeley, he led the Berkeley RISC research project in the 1980s. Its reduced instruction set ideas shaped more than four decades of processor design and directly contributed to the creation of RISC-V. He also introduced the RAID storage architecture concept and co-authored the seminal textbooks Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach and Computer Organization and Design with Hennessy, educating generations of computer architecture talent worldwide. In 2018, he helped launch the RISC-V International Open Source Laboratory (RIOS Lab) to advance global academic and industry collaboration around the RISC-V ecosystem."

  - id: meng-zhou
    name: Meng Zhou
    position: Executive Secretary-General, CESA RISC-V Working Committee
    talk_title: The Inflection Point for Scaling China's RISC-V Industry and Standardization in Practice
    abstract: |-
      China's RISC-V industry is reaching an inflection point as it moves from ecosystem development toward commercial scale. According to research by the working committee, China shipped more than 3 billion RISC-V chips in 2025, accounting for roughly half of global shipments. The combined output value of chips, modules, and complete systems reached approximately RMB 170 billion, up more than 35% year over year. China has become a leading application market, although domestic shipments still concentrate on lower- and mid-range IoT chips and the transition toward high-performance and AI applications is only beginning.

      This talk examines three areas: the industry's inflection point, including the rapid growth of benchmark product submissions and the software ecosystem's shift from basic operation to usability; standardization in practice, including 35 group standards developed through translation and reference adoption; and the integration of AI with Shenzhen's industrial strengths, where coordinated policies have enabled volume deployments in smart highways, water systems, connected appliances, wearables, and server systems.

      The talk also proposes developing Shenzhen as a RISC-V standards validation hub that connects standards, evaluation, deployment scenarios, and scale, while strengthening links between the global ecosystem and China's industry and market.
    summary: An industry standards specialist working across integrated-circuit platforms, standards development, and RISC-V industry research.
    bio: Meng Zhou is Executive Secretary-General of the CESA RISC-V Working Committee. She holds a doctorate in engineering and is a senior engineer. She has worked on the national integrated-circuit public service platform and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology's IP core standards working group, contributed to multiple industry standards, and led or participated in research commissioned by national and provincial authorities.
